# Free Source Map for TRPG Design

This is a curated syllabus, not an endorsement of one school of design. Sources are selected because they are freely readable and expose a useful design approach. “Openly readable” does not always mean “licensed for copying”; verify the specific license before reusing text.

## Start here

| Source | What to study | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| [The Alexandrian — Game Structures](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/15126/roleplaying-games/game-structures) | The difference between a resolution mechanic and a procedure that tells players what to do next | Prevents building a dice engine with no playable activity loop |
| [Lumpley Games — Powered by the Apocalypse, Part 1](https://lumpley.games/2019/12/30/powered-by-the-apocalypse-part-1/) | How real rules give momentum to fictional conversation | A concise model of fiction–mechanics interaction from Apocalypse World’s designer |
| [Storybrewers — Playtesting Your Tabletop RPG](https://storybrewersroleplaying.com/2019/11/25/playtesting-your-tabletop-rpg/) | Staged, continuous playtesting and actionable observations | Establishes that testing is design work, not final polish |
| [AnyDice documentation](https://anydice.com/docs/) | Exact outcome distributions for dice mechanics | Replaces probability intuition with measurable curves |

## Reading method

For every system, fill this comparison card:

- **Promise:** What experience does the game explicitly promise?
- **Core activity:** What do characters repeatedly do?
- **Player decisions:** What choices recur?
- **Authority:** Who establishes facts, calls rules, and chooses consequences?
- **Resolution:** What triggers mechanics and what outcomes exist?
- **Pressure:** Why can players not wait or retry forever?
- **Consequences:** How does trouble create further play?
- **Characters:** What makes two characters play differently?
- **Campaign:** What changes over multiple sessions?
- **Facilitator support:** What concrete procedures generate and run situations?
- **Text lesson:** What is taught especially well or poorly?
- **Borrowable principle:** An idea we can restate in our own design language.

## Source discipline

- Record the URL, author/publisher, access date, and license for anything we may reuse.
- Quote sparingly in research notes and prefer paraphrase.
- Do not assume “free download” permits redistribution or adaptation.
- Do not assume mechanics being legally distinct from expression makes trademarks, setting material, art, or exact rules text reusable.
- Keep inspiration, licensed adaptation, and copied material in separate categories.
- Obtain legal advice before commercial release if licensing or ownership is uncertain.

## Research gaps to fill after the premise is known

The following cannot be researched efficiently until we choose the intended experience:

- genre-specific scenario structures;
- combat or non-combat tactical models;
- mystery and information design;
- social and relationship mechanics;
- solo/GMless requirements;
- campaign economy and advancement;
- historical/cultural research;
- layout and accessibility targets;
- market and comparable-game analysis.
